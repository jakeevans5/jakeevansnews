# What I'm Focused on Right Now
### Learning Before Forming Opinions

<img src="/assets/images/series-5/part-1.webp" alt="What I'm Focused on Right Now" class="centered-photo" />

Before I’m sworn in, my focus is fairly narrow and very intentional.

Right now, I’m spending most of my time learning the issues that are already in front of the city—and then digging into why certain processes exist the way they do.

When I come across a policy, a procedure, or a repeated pattern of how things are handled, my first question isn’t whether it’s right or wrong. It’s where did this come from? Is this a common municipal practice? Is it something that developed over time out of habit or tradition? Or is it tied directly to the city charter, an ordinance, or a legal requirement?

That distinction matters more than it might seem. Some processes exist because they have to. Others exist because they’ve always been done that way. And some exist because, at some point, they solved a real problem—even if that context isn’t obvious anymore.

At this stage, I’m not trying to challenge or change anything. I don’t have authority, and I’m careful not to act like I do. The goal is simply to understand enough that I’m not walking in blind.

I also don’t have a rigid rule for when I stop researching a topic. Most of the time, it comes down to whether I feel confident that my question has actually been answered. If I understand what the process is, why it exists, and where it comes from, that’s usually enough for now.

I’m not aiming for perfection or total mastery before day one. I’m aiming to reduce avoidable “newbie” mistakes—the kind that happen when someone skips the learning phase and assumes they’ll just figure it out later.

This window before swearing-in isn’t about action. It’s about preparation, context, and discipline. And for me, that starts with learning before forming opinions.
