# What I Expect to Be Held To
### Standards That Don’t Change With Authority

<img src="/assets/images/series-5/part-5.jpg" alt="How I Think About Being Wrong and Course-Correcting" class="centered-photo" />

When I think about what people should reasonably be able to hold me to—especially before I’m sworn in—I don’t think in terms of outcomes or decisions.

I think in terms of standards.

Specifically, the Army Values: Loyalty, Duty, Respect, Selfless Service, Honor, Integrity, and Personal Courage.

Those values weren’t situational when I learned them, and they aren’t situational now. They don’t depend on a title, a vote, or a role. They’re standards I’ve carried with me long after my time in uniform, and they continue to shape how I try to conduct myself.

In this window between election and oath, those values guide how I approach preparation. Loyalty means being faithful to the responsibilities of the role, not to personal preference. Duty means doing the work to understand issues fully, even when it’s tedious or uncomfortable. Respect means listening carefully—especially when I disagree.

Selfless service reminds me that preparation isn’t about recognition. Honor and integrity shape how I handle information, questions, and uncertainty. And personal courage shows up quietly—by being willing to say “I don’t know yet,” to ask questions, and to resist easy answers when they aren’t supported.

These are not promises about what I’ll do in office. They’re standards of conduct I believe are fair to expect of me now, before any authority formally begins.

If people are going to hold me to anything in this moment, I believe it should be that.
