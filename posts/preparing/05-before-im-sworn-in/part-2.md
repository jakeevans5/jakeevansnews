# What I'm Intentionally Not Doing Yet
### Understanding Without Assuming Authority

Before I’m sworn in, I don’t think in terms of acting or holding anything back—because there is no authority to act with.

Right now, I’m still just a citizen. No different than anyone else.

Preparation doesn’t create authority, and interest doesn’t either. Until something formally changes, there is none. Even when authority eventually exists, it won’t be personal or open-ended. It will be clearly defined—by the charter, and in some cases, by the code. That clarity matters, because it reinforces that authority comes from structure, not intent.

Most of my questions don’t come from trying to influence anything. They come from listening.

A lot of them start at council meetings. I’ll hear a discussion unfold or a question asked and wonder why that specific question mattered, or what it was referencing. Those moments don’t give me answers—they open doors. They lead me deeper into how the city actually runs and how government functions behind the scenes.

The same thing happens when I hear comments from citizens—or really anyone. If something doesn’t quite line up or doesn’t hit the right mental chord, I take that as a signal to pause and research it further. Not to argue it, but to understand it.

My approach is simple: I look for empirical evidence first. Assumptions tend to show up when evidence doesn’t. If the evidence exists, the answer usually becomes clearer. If it doesn’t, the next step is to ask questions of the people who should know—or who can point to where that evidence ought to be.

When the evidence still isn’t there, I treat that as a gray area. In those cases, I look outward to see how other cities in the area have handled similar situations—not because their approach is automatically right, but because precedent and comparison can provide useful context.

If that still doesn’t provide clarity, I listen. I talk to people and try to understand what the general consensus is.

That process isn’t about forcing answers. It’s about understanding the system as it exists, without assuming authority that isn’t there. Especially in government, gray areas are where caution matters most—and that’s exactly why structure, process, and legal guidance exist.

This stage isn’t about decisions. It’s about posture. And for me, that posture starts with understanding before authority ever begins.
