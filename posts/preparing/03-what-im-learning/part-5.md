# What Has Surprised Me Most So Far
### Why understanding the system matters before offering solutions

<img src="/assets/images/series-3/part-5.jpg" alt="How I Think About Being Wrong and Course-Correcting" class="centered-photo" />

When I look back over everything I’ve been learning, I wouldn’t describe any of it as shocking.

Nothing has felt dramatic.
Nothing has felt like a sudden revelation.

What has surprised me is how complex and layered local government actually is.

On the surface, many things seem straightforward. Who decides? Who pays? Why does this take so long? From the outside, it’s easy to assume there are simple answers—or at least simpler ones than what we experience.

What I’m realizing now is that those assumptions don’t hold up very well.

Local government is made up of structures, roles, funding rules, legal requirements, historical decisions, and processes that all overlap. Very few decisions exist in isolation. Most are shaped by constraints that aren’t obvious unless you take the time to understand how they fit together.

That complexity doesn’t mean the system is broken. It means it’s designed to balance competing needs—transparency, accountability, public input, legality, and long-term responsibility. Those balances create friction. They slow things down. They complicate what might otherwise feel like “common sense” solutions.

One of the biggest shifts for me has been realizing how much harm can come from jumping to conclusions.

When we assume intent instead of understanding process, we add noise instead of clarity. We feed frustration into a system that already carries a lot of moving parts. And we often end up talking past one another, even when everyone involved wants a better outcome.

I know now that reacting quickly—without understanding the structure behind an issue—doesn’t simplify anything. It just adds pressure to something that already needs careful handling.

That doesn’t mean questions shouldn’t be asked. They should. But they work best when they’re grounded in curiosity rather than certainty.

This series hasn’t given me answers to everything. What it’s given me is a better appreciation for how much there is to learn—and how important it is to understand the system before trying to fix it.

If there’s one takeaway that keeps coming back for me, it’s this:
local government isn’t simple, and treating it like it is only makes it harder to navigate.

Understanding first changes everything that comes after.
