# What Service Means to Me
### Listening first, understanding what’s needed, and showing up

<img src="/assets/images/part-3.webp" alt="What Service Means to Me" class="centered-photo" />

When I use the word service, I mean something very simple.

Service starts with me.

It starts with being willing to step outside of myself and focus on someone else’s needs. Not my preferences. Not my assumptions. Not what I think would look helpful from the outside.

For me, service is mostly about helping others — and helping in the way that’s actually needed.

Over time, I’ve learned that the first step in serving well is asking a basic question:  
**Do you need help solving something, or do you need someone to listen?**

Those are very different needs, and confusing them can do more harm than good.

Sometimes people don’t need solutions. They need to be heard. They need someone to take the time to listen, understand what’s going on, and acknowledge the situation without trying to fix it immediately. Other times, there is a problem that needs solving, and help means stepping in and doing real work.

Service works best when you take the time to figure out which one it is.

I’ve learned this the hard way. Earlier in my life, I was quick to jump in and “do” things myself, assuming my way was the right way — or at least the fastest way. What experience taught me is that good intentions don’t automatically lead to good outcomes. My way isn’t always the best way, and sometimes it isn’t even the right way.

That’s why listening matters so much to me.

Service doesn’t mean doing everything. It doesn’t mean taking over. It means showing up, understanding what’s truly needed, and then doing your part — not more, not less.

To me, showing up means being present, doing the work you’re responsible for, and respecting the roles and perspectives of others involved. It means learning from people who are closer to the issue than you are and taking their input seriously.

Helping works best when you let the people who need help tell you what’s going on, instead of assuming you already know.

That’s what service looks like to me: listening first, acting with intention, and doing the work once you understand what’s actually needed.
