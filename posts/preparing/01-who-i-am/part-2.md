# Why I Care About Neosho

<img src="/assets/images/who-i-am.jpg" alt="Why I Care About Neosho" class="centered-photo" />

I’ve been thinking a lot about what it means to say a place is “home.”

For some people, that word is tied to friend groups, social circles, or nostalgia. For me, it’s simpler and more grounded than that.

Neosho is home because it’s where I was born, where I grew up, where I went to school and church, and where I eventually chose to come back. I spent time away, lived elsewhere, and still returned here—not out of obligation, but because this is the place that feels permanent to me.

When I say “these are my people,” I don’t mean it in a social sense. I don’t define belonging by how often I’m invited places or how many close friendships I have locally. I know a lot of people here. I have acquaintances. People are friendly to me, and I’m friendly back. That’s enough for me.

What connects me to Neosho is something different.

It’s the shared expectation that when someone needs help, you help. That when something affects the community, you don’t shrug and walk away. That responsibility doesn’t belong to “someone else” just because it’s inconvenient.

I’ve always been more analytical than social. I connect to communities through responsibility, through systems, and through outcomes. For me, caring about a place means being willing to show up, to do the work, and to take ownership when decisions have real consequences.

That’s why local government matters to me.

Decisions made at the city level affect daily life quickly and directly. They’re not abstract. They show up in how services are delivered, how resources are managed, and how problems are addressed—or ignored. Real impact happens close to home, often quietly, without recognition.

I’m not drawn to scale or status. I’m drawn to where responsibility actually lives.

Neosho isn’t just where I live. It’s where I’m willing to stay engaged, ask questions, and take responsibility for outcomes—because when you serve locally, you don’t get to walk away from the results.

This place matters to me because it’s home in the truest sense of the word: not because it’s comfortable, but because it’s mine to care for.
