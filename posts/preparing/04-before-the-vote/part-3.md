# How Board and Commission Service Shaped My Approach
### Listening longer and preparing better

![Series 4 – Part 3 header image](/assets/images/series-4/part-3.jpg)

Serving on boards and commissions didn’t change who I am in meetings as much as it deepened habits I already valued.

One of the biggest things it reinforced was an appreciation for different perspectives. Being part of those discussions made it clear that people often arrive at the same issue from very different places. Disagreement isn’t unusual — and it doesn’t automatically mean someone is wrong or acting in bad faith.

That experience also strengthened the importance of pausing and listening. I try not to respond quickly, especially before I fully understand what someone is saying. If something isn’t clear, I make a point to ask questions that help me understand the other person better — not questions designed to steer an answer or catch someone off guard.

I’ve never been interested in “gotcha” moments. For me, questions are about clarity, not leverage. If I ask something, it’s because I want to make sure I’m understanding the issue correctly.

Serving on boards and commissions also reinforced the importance of preparation. Reading materials ahead of time matters. It changes the quality of discussion and helps ensure that questions are thoughtful rather than reactive.

Overall, those experiences didn’t flip a switch — they sharpened it. They reinforced the value of listening, preparation, and approaching discussions with the intent to understand before responding.
