# How I Prepare Questions
### Understanding the basics before anything moves forward

When I decide to ask a question, it’s usually because I’m trying to fill in basic gaps — not challenge intent or push an outcome.

As I read through an agenda item or packet, I’m mentally walking through the fundamentals: who, what, when, where, why, and how. If I can’t answer those for myself from what’s written, that’s when a question forms.

I want to understand what is actually being proposed and why it’s being brought forward now. I’m looking for when a change would take effect if it’s approved and whether that timing matters. I want to know who will be affected by it and where those effects will show up — whether that’s operationally, financially, or for the public.

I also pay attention to how something is supposed to work in practice. If the packet explains the idea but not the execution, that’s a signal that clarification is needed.

None of this is about catching mistakes or creating friction. It’s about making sure the basics are understood before anything moves forward. If I can clearly answer those questions, I usually don’t need to ask anything at all. If I can’t, that’s when I know a question belongs in the meeting.
