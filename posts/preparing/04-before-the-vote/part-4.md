# What I Focus On Before Meetings
### Listening for understanding, not conclusions

<img src="/assets/images/series-4/part-4.jpg" alt="Empty Room" class="centered-photo" />

Before I walk into a meeting where I’m serving on a board or commission, my focus isn’t on what I want to say — it’s on what I need to hear.

I listen closely during the initial presentation of an item for answers to questions the agenda or packet may not have fully covered. Often, those clarifications come out naturally as staff or presenters walk through the material. When they do, there’s usually no need to ask anything further.

If those gaps aren’t addressed during the presentation, I wait until questions are appropriate and ask then. That helps keep the discussion focused and respectful of the process.

I also make a deliberate effort to keep an open mind going into meetings. I think about how easy it is to misread tone — the same way a text message can sound very different to the sender and the recipient. Keeping that in mind helps me avoid forming conclusions too early and allows me to better understand what’s actually being said when discussion begins.

Approaching meetings this way helps me stay grounded. I’m listening for understanding first, and only stepping in when clarification is genuinely needed.
